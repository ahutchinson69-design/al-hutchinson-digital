Résumé Workflow Worksheet

Structured prompts for describing your clinical experience accurately and specifically — without inflating it. AI can help you find the words. It cannot verify your record.

The rule: only put in language you can stand behind if someone asks you about it directly, in an interview, under questions.

The workflow

  1. 1List the raw facts first, no adjectives. For each role, write what you actually did, day to day, in plain language — before you touch a prompt.
  2. 2Quantify only what you can back up. Caseload, census, years, certifications held, number of people trained. If you don't have a real number, use a range you know is true, or leave it out entirely.
  3. 3Turn your plain-language notes into a prompt. Use the templates on this page — paste your own facts in, don't describe them from memory a second time.
  4. 4Compare the draft against your list from step 1. Anything in the draft that isn't in your original list gets cut, not softened.
  5. 5Read it aloud as if you were being interviewed. If a line doesn't sound like something you'd defend under a follow-up question, rewrite it.

Prompt templates

A — Turn a task into a bullet

I need a resume bullet describing this responsibility: [describe the task in your own words]. Keep it to one line, use an action verb, and do not add outcomes or numbers I have not given you.

B — Tighten existing language

Rewrite this bullet to be more specific and concise, without changing any facts or adding claims I did not state: [paste your bullet]

C — Match a job posting honestly

Here is a job description: [paste]. Here is my actual experience: [describe it yourself]. Tell me which of my real experience is most relevant to highlight. Do not suggest adding experience I don't have.

Checking your draft, and space to work

Specific beats impressive

Vague — an AI default you should edit outSpecific — what you should end up with
"Responsible for patient care in a busy clinical setting.""Managed direct care for a daily caseload across [your actual setting], coordinating with an interdisciplinary team."
"Highly skilled, results-driven healthcare professional."[Delete. Let the specific facts make this case instead.]
"Improved patient outcomes through proactive intervention."[Only keep an outcome claim if you can point to how it was actually measured.]

A hiring manager in healthcare can tell the difference between a real number and a padded one. Specificity reads as credibility; adjectives read as filler.

Before you submit — check for these AI habits

  • Outcome claims with no source ("reduced errors by X%") that you can't actually trace to a measurement.
  • Adjectives doing the work nouns should do — "dedicated," "results-driven," "highly skilled."
  • Numbers that sound suspiciously round, or that you added just to have a number.
  • A responsibility listed that you'd actually have to explain differently if asked in person.
  • Anything copied straight from the job posting rather than describing your own experience.
